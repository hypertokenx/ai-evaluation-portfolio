# Clear Explanation Example: Confirmation Bias

Confirmation bias is the tendency to seek, interpret, and remember information in ways that favor beliefs we already hold.

## Simple Example

Suppose someone believes a particular diet is the best way to lose weight. They may pay more attention to success stories that support the diet and give less weight to studies or personal experiences showing mixed or negative results.

## Why It Matters

Confirmation bias can contribute to poor decisions because it makes contradictory evidence easier to dismiss. It can affect everyday decisions, workplace judgments, political beliefs, scientific interpretation, and other situations where people must evaluate competing information.

Recognizing the bias does not require assuming that every belief is wrong. The useful question is whether we are applying the same evidentiary standard to information that supports our view and information that challenges it.
